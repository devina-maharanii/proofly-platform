# Proofly Brand Strategy and Design System Direction

## Strategy scope

This document defines the verbal and visual foundation for Proofly before final component and interface implementation. It is a strategy contract, not a completed visual identity, logo, UI kit, or application feature set. The brand must feel **trustworthy before impressive** and must make the evidence chain understandable without turning a person's future into an opaque score.

The approved product direction remains the first-release wedge of junior and early-career software developers, startups and small product teams, and qualified reviewers. The intended network is global, while initial production availability remains subject to the approved market register.

## Brand essence

> **Credibility earned through evidence.**

Proofly should make credibility feel observable, explainable, and human-accountable. The brand is not about appearing authoritative; it is about showing how a real piece of work was made visible, reviewed, understood, and connected to a fair opportunity.

## Brand promise

> **Proofly helps people earn trusted opportunities by making real work visible, reviewable, and understandable.**

The promise is specific to verified-work context and trusted opportunity. It does not promise employment, acceptance, a particular score, a successful outcome, or a universal measure of ability. “Trusted” describes a direction supported by evidence, review, explanation, and user control; it is not a guarantee.

## Audience positioning

| Audience | Positioning | Value in one glance | What the brand must avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Talent** | Proofly is a fairer way to show what a person can build, with real work, qualified review, useful feedback, and control over what becomes public. | “Your work can be understood on its own terms, not reduced to pedigree or an opaque score.” | Deficit language, gamified ranking, career guarantees, or treating a person as a candidate record. |
| **Company** | Proofly gives startups and small product teams a clearer evidence signal before human hiring judgment and a bounded path to a paid trial. | “Inspect relevant work, understand the signal, then decide with your own judgment.” | Replacing hiring judgment, promising a perfect hire, or implying that one score settles suitability. |
| **Reviewer** | Proofly gives experienced practitioners a structured, transparent, and accountable way to evaluate serious work and help talent improve. | “Make expert judgment useful without becoming an opaque gatekeeper.” | Hidden criteria, pay-for-positive outcomes, status theater, or calling machine assistance human review. |

The audiences are distinct, but the brand promise is shared: make real work visible, reviewable, and understandable. A `Person` may act in more than one role; messaging must name the active role and action rather than imply separate human identities.

## Positioning statement

> **For junior and early-career software developers, startups, small product teams, and qualified reviewers, Proofly is the evidence-centered bridge from real work to trusted opportunity: it connects a specific submission to qualified human review, explainable proof, and a bounded paid trial without becoming another resume profile, generic job board, open proposal marketplace, or opaque talent score.**

The positioning follows the product's proof-to-opportunity loop: choose a relevant challenge, submit real work, receive qualified human review, record eligible proof, discover through evidence, begin a bounded compensated trial, and create an auditable outcome. The loop is a product direction and research hypothesis, not a claim of validated market behavior.

## Personality

Proofly's personality should guide both marketing copy and product microcopy. Each trait has a boundary so it does not become a generic tone adjective.

| Trait | We sound like | We do not sound like |
|---|---|---|
| **Precise, not cold** | Specific about what was reviewed, what is visible, and what happens next | Legalistic, robotic, or dismissive |
| **Confident, not loud** | Calm about the value of evidence and clear about limits | Boastful, dominant, or breathless |
| **Technical, not cryptic** | Comfortable with code, rubrics, provenance, and state without assuming specialist knowledge | Jargon-heavy, tool-worshipping, or needlessly abstract |
| **Encouraging, not sentimental** | Respectful of effort and useful about improvement | Inspirational filler, pity, or emotional pressure |
| **Selective, not elitist** | Clear about qualification, scope, and review quality | Gatekeeping by pedigree, hidden criteria, or status |
| **Transparent, not performative** | Shows sources, uncertainty, review context, and limits | Trust theater, badges without context, or decorative claims |
| **Human, not casual** | Treats users as capable adults making consequential choices | Overfamiliar, slang-heavy, or patronizing |

## Voice principles

1. **Use concrete verbs.** Prefer `build`, `submit`, `review`, `explain`, `compare`, `publish`, `apply`, and `start` over abstract verbs such as `unlock`, `transform`, or `revolutionize`.
2. **Explain verification.** Say what was reviewed, by whom, against which rubric, and what remains uncertain. Do not use `verified` as a decorative adjective.
3. **Replace hype with evidence.** Lead with the work, context, source, review, and next action rather than market size, status, or superlatives.
4. **Treat users as capable adults.** Give enough context for informed consent and choice. Do not hide a critical requirement behind an icon, score, or vague label.
5. **Make uncertainty visible.** Use `not yet reviewed`, `pending`, `restricted`, `source not available`, or other precise status language when applicable.
6. **Keep humans accountable.** Name the human reviewer and human company decision where the product context requires it. AI assistance must be labeled as assistance.
7. **Never promise a job or guaranteed outcome.** Proof can support an opportunity; it cannot guarantee employment, acceptance, fairness of every outcome, or future performance.
8. **Keep paid work fair and bounded.** Use `paid trial`, `scope`, `compensation`, and `acceptance criteria`; never soften unpaid production work into a friendly-sounding trial.

### Preferred language

- **Build work people can trust.**
- **Show the decisions behind the result.**
- **Review the work. Understand the signal.**
- **Hire from evidence, then start with a fair trial.**
- **This proof is based on a submitted work version and a completed human review.**
- **AI assistance is advisory; a person makes the decision.**
- **Your proof is private until you choose what to publish.**
- **No review is complete yet. You can revise and resubmit.**

### Avoid language

- Unlock your limitless potential.
- The future of talent is here.
- AI-powered hiring revolution.
- Guaranteed career success.
- Best-in-class talent, no matter what.
- One score tells the whole story.
- Get hired instantly.
- Trust the algorithm.

These examples are guardrails, not a campaign or final UI copy library. Legal, payment, safety, consent, proof, and review-critical language requires product and policy review before publishing and human review for every enabled locale.

## Messaging hierarchy

Every brand surface should move from meaning to evidence to an appropriate next action. The hierarchy prevents a striking headline from making a claim the product cannot support.

| Level | Message job | Required content | Example direction |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Essence** | State the belief behind the product | Credibility earned through evidence. |
| 2 | **Promise** | State the specific value for people and opportunity | Make real work visible, reviewable, and understandable so people can earn trusted opportunities. |
| 3 | **Mechanism** | Explain how the promise becomes credible | A real submission, qualified human review, clear evidence, user-controlled visibility, and a bounded paid path. |
| 4 | **Role value** | Make the relevance concrete for one audience | Talent proves specific ability; companies inspect relevant evidence; reviewers make judgment accountable. |
| 5 | **Proof and limits** | Show what is verified and what is not | Name the work version, rubric, reviewer, source, status, uncertainty, and visibility boundary. |
| 6 | **Next action** | Ask for one safe, understandable step | Explore a challenge, inspect the evidence, accept a review, or review the trial terms. |

### Message architecture by surface

| Surface | Primary audience | Lead message | Supporting evidence | Safe next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing site | Mixed public audience | Real work should be easier to understand before opportunity decisions. | Explain the review chain and the limits of proof without fake logos, testimonials, metrics, or stories. | Explore how it works or get started. |
| Talent profile/proof | Talent and company | This proof shows a specific piece of work and its review context. | Submission version, skills, rubric context, reviewer, source references, status, and visibility. | Inspect the proof, contact through the approved path, or revise privately. |
| Company discovery | Company | Find relevant evidence before making a human hiring decision. | Work context, proof provenance, explanation, uncertainty, and fair-trial terms. | Inspect evidence, shortlist, or invite a bounded paid trial. |
| Reviewer workspace | Reviewer | Make a clear, accountable evaluation of real work. | Rubric, conflict status, source material, feedback, and decision state. | Accept, recuse, request changes, or finalize a review. |
| Status and error copy | Active role | Tell the person what happened and what can be done next. | Specific state, reason where safe, ownership, retry/recovery path, and privacy boundary. | Revise, retry, request help, or wait for the named next step. |

No message hierarchy should use a universal score, fabricated social proof, unsupported market claim, hidden activity, or AI confidence as a substitute for evidence.

## Visual direction

The visual system should feel like a premium precision-editorial developer tool: clear enough for a serious hiring decision, calm enough for dense review work, and distinct enough to avoid generic SaaS patterns.

| Element | Direction | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Cool graphite and fog surfaces with quiet elevation | Use near-black graphite rather than pure black and cool off-white rather than pure white. |
| **Brand accent** | One controlled electric cobalt accent | Cobalt carries primary action and brand signal; semantic colors are reserved for actual success, warning, error, and information. |
| **Typography** | Geist Sans for display/interface; Geist Mono for technical values, code, identifiers, and evidence context | Use a restrained type scale, strong hierarchy, and short headlines. Do not use a decorative serif by default. |
| **Composition** | Asymmetric split layouts, evidence timelines, editorial grids, and purposeful narrative space on marketing surfaces | Do not use three identical feature cards, decorative metric walls, or fake dashboard rectangles. |
| **Product density** | Calm, structured information density for profiles, review, applications, messaging, and payment-critical work | Preserve primary action, progressive disclosure, readable tables only when comparison requires them, and useful empty states. |
| **Material** | Quiet surfaces, whitespace, grouping, dividers, and a documented radius system | No excessive glassmorphism, neon glow, decorative color noise, or AI-purple gradient as the default visual language. |
| **Evidence center** | Put the work, provenance, review context, status, and next action at the visual center | Do not make badges, avatars, popularity, or a single score the visual center of credibility. |

The same direction must support a spacious marketing site and a dense product app. Marketing can use higher compositional variance; product workflows use lower variance and higher information density. Both use the same palette, typography, semantics, contrast, and evidence-first hierarchy.

## Motion direction

Motion has a product reason only when it explains evidence becoming verified, acknowledges a completed review, clarifies a state change, or helps a person understand sequence. Motion must not distract from work quality, hide a status transition, create false urgency, or imply certainty that the data does not support.

| Motion use | Product reason | Default behavior | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proof timeline reveal | Explain the ordered evidence and review chain | Short, restrained transform/opacity transition | Show the complete timeline immediately with the same hierarchy. |
| Review completion acknowledgement | Confirm that a human review was recorded | Small state feedback after the server confirms success | Use a static status change and text confirmation. |
| State transition | Make pending, requested changes, verified, published, or revoked understandable | Animate only the affected status region | Replace animation with a clear status label, timestamp, and next action. |
| Loading and progress | Communicate waiting without fake precision | Skeleton/indeterminate treatment where duration is unknown | Static loading text and accessible progress semantics. |
| Marketing story transition | Connect real work to understandable proof | One purposeful narrative transition | Keep all steps visible in a static composition. |

Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and reduced-transparency preferences. Animate transform and opacity rather than layout properties. Do not use motion to reward activity, pressure a decision, or make AI output feel more authoritative.

## Naming risks and working-name status

**[Decision]** `Proofly` remains a **working product name** pending trademark, domain, pronunciation, language, and user-understanding checks. This phase does not approve a final name or logo.

| Risk | Why it matters | Validation needed |
|---|---|---|
| `Proof` may imply certainty | People may read “proof” as absolute truth rather than bounded evidence with human review and limits. | Test comprehension across talent, companies, and reviewers; explain what a proof does and does not establish. |
| `-ly` may feel generic or tool-like | The suffix may not communicate a serious, technical, global trust product in every locale. | Test recall, credibility, pronunciation, spelling, and role-specific associations. |
| International pronunciation and translation | English-first does not mean every audience will pronounce or interpret the name consistently. | Check pronunciation, negative associations, transliteration, searchability, and locale expansion. |
| Trademark and domain availability | Legal ownership and discoverability are separate from product desirability. | Complete professional trademark, domain, and collision checks before public commitment. |
| Naming can overpromise opportunity | The name or tagline must not imply guaranteed employment, identity assurance, or automatic verification. | Review all name lockups and taglines against the product boundary and legal/policy review. |

Until those checks are complete, use `Proofly` consistently as the working name, avoid claims that it is final, and never invent a logo, customer mark, testimonial, metric, or user story to make the name feel established.

## Accessibility commitments

Accessibility is a brand commitment because an evidence-centered product cannot ask people to trust a signal they cannot read, navigate, understand, or control.

1. **Meet WCAG AA contrast expectations.** Graphite, fog, cobalt, and semantic colors must maintain readable contrast in light and dark themes. Color is never the only carrier of status, verification, warning, error, or review outcome.
2. **Make every interaction keyboard usable.** Provide visible focus indicators, logical focus order, semantic controls, skip/navigation landmarks, and no keyboard traps across marketing, discovery, review, messaging, application, payment, and settings surfaces.
3. **Use semantic and understandable structure.** Use a meaningful heading hierarchy, labels, descriptions, status announcements, table headers, error associations, and plain language. Do not hide critical proof or consent requirements behind unexplained icons.
4. **Respect motion and transparency preferences.** Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and reduced-transparency settings; every motion concept above has an immediate non-animated equivalent.
5. **Support zoom, mobile, and assistive technology.** Preserve hierarchy at text zoom, mobile widths, touch targets, screen readers, and keyboard-only use. Dense workflows may become focused mobile flows rather than compressed desktop layouts.
6. **Design for localization.** Use translation keys, allow text expansion and long names, support pluralization and right-to-left layouts, format dates/times/currency by locale, and keep proof, review, payment, safety, consent, and dispute terminology human-reviewed.
7. **Do not infer identity or ability.** Browser locale, device data, language fluency, assistive technology, or visual presentation must not be used to infer identity, work eligibility, skill, or trustworthiness.
8. **Make privacy and limits perceivable.** Public/private/restricted states, consent, source references, uncertainty, review status, and correction paths must be available as text and accessible semantics, not only color, animation, or visual badges.
9. **Test the brand system in context.** Later design QA must cover six viewport bands, text zoom, contrast, touch targets, focus, reduced motion, long content, locale expansion, RTL, screen readers, and critical proof/review/payment/safety copy.

## Guardrails and non-goals

The brand does not include final UI implementation, a final logo, fake social proof, customer logos, testimonials, market metrics, user stories, generic startup slogans, or a visual treatment that makes AI appear to verify people. It does not turn Proofly into a social feed, job board, generic marketplace, or opaque scoring product.

AI may be mentioned only as approved assistive support when the use case, source, uncertainty, model/version, human approval point, and correction path are clear. “AI-powered” is not a brand promise. The product promise remains real work → qualified human review → explainable proof → bounded paid opportunity.

## Approval and handoff

Before final component work, the product, design, accessibility, research, security, and policy owners must approve the promise, role positioning, voice rules, visual tokens, motion fallbacks, working-name checks, and accessibility test plan. The approved design-system artifacts must preserve this product promise and must not invent a new visual personality.

## Radius, borders, and elevation

Use one geometry language across the system. Product surfaces use 12px–16px radius; controls use 8px radius; pills are reserved for true status or compact filters. Do not mix random rounded and sharp geometry.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| `radius-control` | 8px | Buttons, inputs, selects, tabs, and compact interactive controls. |
| `radius-surface-sm` | 12px | Small grouped surface, status panel, or row container. |
| `radius-surface` | 16px | Main product surface, modal, drawer, or marketing content block. |
| `radius-pill` | 999px | Status badge or compact filter only; never for every control. |
| `border-hairline` | 1px | Structural line using `--line`; not a disabled-state replacement. |

Elevation is quiet and functional. Prefer boundaries, whitespace, and background contrast before shadows.

| Token | Starting treatment | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `elevation-0` | No shadow | Page, inline content, and evidence timeline. |
| `elevation-1` | `0 1px 2px rgb(15 23 42 / 0.08)` | Raised surface or sticky utility bar. |
| `elevation-2` | `0 8px 24px rgb(15 23 42 / 0.12)` | Modal, drawer, or focused overlay. |
| `elevation-focus` | No elevation change; use a visible focus ring | Keyboard focus; never remove the focus indicator. |

Dark-theme elevation must preserve boundaries through surface contrast and line tokens rather than a heavy black shadow. No glass effect is used in dense product workflows.

## Proof, evidence, review, and status patterns

**Evidence pattern:** show the work first, then provenance, then review context, then limits and the next action. Use an ordered timeline and relationship lines when they represent real relationships. Never make an isolated badge, avatar, arbitrary score, contribution count, or decorative metric wall the primary trust signal.

**Proof pattern:** a proof surface must identify the exact submission/version, relevant skill, rubric/review context, reviewer, status, visibility, and outcome where available. `Verified` means the defined human-review chain passed; it does not mean identity assurance, guaranteed ability, or future performance. Public proof must be visibly distinguishable from a private draft, pending verification, or revoked record.

**Review pattern:** show rubric criteria, source evidence, conflict state, reviewer identity/context according to policy, feedback, decision status, and correction path. Human review is distinct from AI analysis. AI assistance is labeled, source-linked, uncertain where applicable, and never styled as an authoritative final review.

**Status pattern:** lifecycle status uses a canonical text label, optional icon/pattern, timestamp or last-updated context, and an action or explanation where useful. Status colors reinforce meaning but never carry meaning alone. Use `draft`, `pending`, `under review`, `changes requested`, `verified`, `published`, `revoked`, `blocked`, and other approved domain states exactly as defined by the domain model; do not invent visually convenient synonyms.

## Motion system

Use `motion/react` for interface transitions where motion is approved. Motion explains evidence becoming verified, a review completion, saved work, a state change, or a readable sequence. It must never manufacture urgency, distract from work quality, hide a status transition, or make AI output appear more authoritative.

| Motion token | Starting value | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| `duration-fast` | 120ms | Small hover/focus acknowledgement and control feedback. |
| `duration-standard` | 180ms | Status, panel, and feedback transitions. |
| `duration-story` | 320ms | Marketing evidence sequence or proof timeline reveal. |
| `ease-standard` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)` | Calm entry/exit without elastic bounce. |

Marketing entry transitions may use opacity and transform. Product motion should acknowledge a server-confirmed state change, review completion, or saved work. There is no perpetual animation unless it communicates a real live status. Do not use scroll listeners or React state for continuous pointer or scroll values. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`: remove non-essential animation, show the complete content immediately, and preserve the same hierarchy, status, and next action. Honor reduced-transparency preferences by replacing translucent surfaces with opaque token surfaces.

## Responsive and mobile rules

- Use a strict single-column collapse below `768px` for asymmetric marketing layouts; do not merely shrink two columns until content becomes unreadable.
- Use `min-h-[100dvh]` for full-height sections; never use `h-screen` where mobile browser chrome can create clipping.
- Keep the primary desktop CTA visible and one line where the content permits; on small screens, preserve the same action priority and allow safe wrapping rather than truncating.
- Preserve evidence hierarchy on mobile: work context, provenance, review status, limits, and next action remain in that order. Do not compress evidence into an arbitrary score or hide relationships in a horizontal overflow trap.
- Collapse wide tables into labelled stacked rows or a deliberate focused comparison view. Do not render an unreadable desktop table inside a narrow viewport.
- Keep touch targets appropriate for mobile, visible focus for keyboard users, and content readable at text zoom. Drawers and modals must have a reachable close action and a safe scroll region.
- Product mobile is first-class for reading, review, messaging, and application. Complex code submission and reviewer comparison may use a focused mobile flow rather than a compressed desktop layout.

## Accessibility and interaction rules

- Meet **WCAG AA** contrast requirements for text, controls, focus indicators, and status pairings in both light and dark themes. Contrast values must be tested, not assumed from token names.
- Every interaction is keyboard operable with a visible focus state, logical focus order, semantic control, and no keyboard trap. Focus must not disappear against graphite, fog, surface, or cobalt backgrounds.
- Use semantic HTML, a meaningful heading order, landmarks, labels, descriptions, table headers, error associations, and accessible status announcements. Icon-only buttons require accessible labels.
- Never communicate status, verification, review outcome, or permission using color alone. Pair color with text, icon, pattern, position, or structural change.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and reduced-transparency preferences. Provide static and opaque fallbacks that preserve content and meaning.
- Preserve accessibility under text zoom, long content, mobile widths, localization, right-to-left layout, screen readers, and keyboard-only use. Do not rely on hover as the only way to expose evidence or an explanation.
- Public/private/restricted proof states, consent, sources, uncertainty, review status, correction paths, and error recovery must be available as readable text and accessible semantics, not only visual badges.
- Keep legal, payment, safety, consent, proof, and review-critical copy human-reviewed in every enabled locale. Use translation keys and do not render raw keys in production.
- Do not infer identity, ability, work eligibility, or trustworthiness from browser locale, device data, language fluency, assistive technology, or visual presentation.

## Marketing versus product surface rules

| Rule | Marketing surface | Product surface |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | Asymmetric split hero, evidence timeline, editorial grid, and one purposeful sticky story section | Grouped panels, rows, timelines, and progressive disclosure with one primary action |
| Variance | `DESIGN_VARIANCE: 7`; use expressive composition with disciplined tokens | `DESIGN_VARIANCE: 4`; use repeatable structures and predictable hierarchy |
| Density | `VISUAL_DENSITY: 3`; generous whitespace and concise copy | `VISUAL_DENSITY: 6`; information-rich but calm, with readable grouping |
| Motion | `MOTION_INTENSITY: 5`; restrained transform/opacity that explains the story | `MOTION_INTENSITY: 3`; only acknowledge a real state, save, review, or sequence change |
| Evidence | Explain why proof is credible without inventing proof, logos, testimonials, metrics, or user stories | Make work, provenance, review, status, limits, and next action more prominent than scores or decoration |
| Copy | Lead with the specific promise and how it works; avoid hype and guarantees | Use direct state, consequence, recovery, ownership, and next-action language |
| Geometry | Editorial whitespace and purposeful surfaces | 12px–16px surfaces, 8px controls, dividers, and restrained elevation |
| Responsive behavior | Collapse to one column below 768px and preserve narrative order | Preserve evidence order and primary action; convert wide comparisons to focused mobile flows |

Do not use default shadcn styling without applying these tokens, states, radii, typography, and accessibility rules. Do not use generic three-card feature rows, decorative grids, cockpit dashboards, fake metrics, AI-purple gradients, excessive glassmorphism, neon glow, or a second component system.

## Phase 07 handoff and QA gate

A future screen may begin only when its route, authorized role, data assumptions, loading/empty/validation/error/success/restricted/offline states, responsive behavior, keyboard/focus behavior, reduced-motion behavior, component tokens, and primary action are documented. Visual QA must exercise light and dark themes, six viewport bands, text zoom, contrast, focus, touch targets, reduced motion/transparency, long content, locale expansion, RTL, and screen-reader semantics.

The design system is complete for Phase 07 when a new Manus task can select semantic colors, typography, spacing, radius, elevation, component states, proof/status patterns, motion fallbacks, and marketing/product rules without inventing a new value or visual personality. This is a design contract, not a claim that those screens or components have been implemented.
